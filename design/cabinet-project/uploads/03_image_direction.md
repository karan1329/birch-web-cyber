# Image direction · the hero image for The Security Read

**Purpose.** This is the brief for the one asset that decides whether the page works. Everything else on the page is type and rules, and type and rules can be fixed in an afternoon. The image cannot.

**Who reads the page.** A bank CISO, a head of IT risk at an NBFC, the person at an MAS-licensed institution who owns security part time on top of a CTO job. They read documents for a living. They have seen every piece of stock security imagery that exists and they discount all of it.

---

## Part 1 · The rules the two references actually obey

Both reference shots are by the same designer and both obey three rules. Everything else about them is surface.

**Rule 1. One hue family. No second colour.**
Axiom is blue at nine lightnesses. Apogee is coral against near-black. Neither image contains a second chroma anywhere. The moment a second colour enters, the image reads as generated. Our hue is ochre `#B8862F` on warm paper and near-black ink. Nothing else. No blue, no green, no red except the single crimson permitted for a severity mark.

**Rule 2. One texture treatment across every element, applied last.**
Apogee wins because a single grain layer sits over a vector arc, a photograph, and a crisp UI mockup, and drags all three onto one surface. Axiom wins because every element has been pushed into the same narrow value range until the composite disappears. Pick one unifier and apply it globally at the end. Do not treat elements individually.

**Rule 3. Negative space is reserved before anything is placed.**
In both shots the type and the UI cards sit exactly where the image is quietest. Nothing is scrimmed, no text has a shadow, no card needs an opaque background. The hole was designed first and the picture was made around it.

There is a fourth rule that only Axiom follows and it is the one that matters most for us.

**Rule 4. A measurement overlay converts a picture into an instrument.**
The dot-and-dash contour bands across Axiom's sky are what make it "observability" rather than "a nice cloud." They have a logic: they follow the form, they vary in density, they read as isobars. This is the single cheapest, highest-leverage device in either reference, and it is the one that transfers most directly to a firm that sells reading and scoring.

## Part 2 · The trap, stated plainly

Cybersecurity is the most degraded visual category on the internet. The following are **banned outright**, whatever the execution quality:

padlocks · shields · hoodies and faceless figures · green matrix rain · binary streams · circuit-board textures · hexagon grids · fingerprint and retina scans · glowing blue tunnels and data corridors · world maps with arcing attack lines · keyholes · firewalls drawn as brick walls · anything with a lens flare · anything with a neon glow · abstract "network node" constellations · a person looking at a screen with reflected code on their glasses.

Every skill in the design library independently bans this category. `operational-enterprise-ai` names it: avoid "glowing AI orbs, particle fields, neon gradients, and generic cyber-security imagery."

**The reframe that gets us out of the trap:** Axiom's subject is not "cloud computing." It is *a thing being measured*. Their product measures cloud infrastructure, so the image is a cloud with measurement marks on it.

Our product does not secure anything. It **reads**. It reads a company's security the way a regulator would, before the regulator does. So the subject is not security. The subject is **the document, the reading of it, or the room where the reading happens**.

That reframe is the whole brief.

## Part 3 · Three directions, in order of preference

### Direction A · The Read under raking light  · RECOMMENDED

**Subject.** A small stack of printed pages on a plain surface, shot from a low oblique angle, maybe twenty degrees above the plane, with the top page slightly offset from the ones beneath. Hard directional light entering from the left at a shallow raking angle, so the paper's surface texture and the slight curl of the top sheet are visible, and so the right half of the frame falls into deep shadow.

**The device.** The type on the page is present but not legible: rendered at a size and focus where it reads as texture, the way a page looks from across a desk. The **only fully resolved, fully black elements in the frame are the redaction bars.** Three or four of them, solid, hard-edged, at different lengths, scattered down the visible page.

That is the image. A document you cannot read, with the parts someone removed rendered as the only certainty in it. For this buyer it is immediately legible and it is not a metaphor anyone else in the category owns.

**Why it beats the alternatives.** It is literally the deliverable. It borrows Axiom's "object embedded in atmosphere" through the raking light dissolving the paper's right edge into shadow, so the subject and the ground share a value range. It borrows Apogee's monumentality by shooting close and low so the page is cropped by the frame and implied to be larger than it. And the redaction bars give the image a single hard, graphic, unfakeable element.

**Composition.**
- Frame ratio `4:5` portrait for the split panel, `3:2` if the panel goes wider. Render at 3× the display size, minimum 2400px on the long edge.
- Stack sits **lower-right**, cropped by the bottom edge and the right edge.
- **Upper-left third is reserved and empty**, falling into shadow. This is where the mono caption or a card sits. Nothing must happen there.
- Horizon: none. Surface only. No table edge, no room, no context. Weightless the way Axiom's sky is weightless.

**Light.** One source, left, shallow, hard enough to throw a defined shadow off the page edges but soft enough not to blow the paper white. Falloff to near-black on the right. No fill light. No second source. No specular highlight anywhere.

**Palette and value targets.**

| element | approx | note |
|---|---|---|
| paper, lit | `#F2EBDF` to `#FBF9F5` | never pure white |
| paper, mid | `#C9C3B8` | the falloff |
| paper, shadow | `#3A3833` | where the page enters shade |
| ground shadow | `#0E1B2C` to `#060B12` | warm-cool near-black |
| redaction bars | `#0D0D0F` | the only true black |
| accent | `#B8862F` | one element only, see below |

**The single ochre element.** Exactly one. Options, pick one: a printed tab or index marker on the edge of the stack, a thin rule under a section heading on the visible page, or a small mark in a margin. It must be small, it must be printed rather than glowing, and it must sit in the lit half.

**Post treatment, applied last, globally.**
- Grain: monochrome, fine, roughly the density of ISO 800 film. Heavier in the shadows than the highlights. This is what stops the image reading as a 3D render.
- Vignette: subtle, corners down maybe eight percent.
- No colour grading beyond a slight warm bias. No teal-and-orange. No bloom.

### Direction B · The table, from above

**Subject.** Directly overhead, flat, no perspective. A dark surface with printed material laid out on it in a loose grid: several stapled documents, one open and turned ninety degrees to the others, a folded regulator's letter, a printed spreadsheet. No hands, no faces, no coffee cups, no laptops, no props that date it.

Everything desaturated to near-monochrome. One ochre element as above. Redaction bars visible on two of the documents.

**The device.** Fine hairline rules drawn between the documents in post, connecting them the way a reviewer's pencil would, with two or three tiny mono labels at the connection points. This is Axiom's measurement overlay applied to a flat lay: it turns "papers on a desk" into "a reading in progress."

**Strength:** it says a real engagement happened, without a stock handshake or a boardroom.
**Risk:** flat lays are heavily stocked and this can slide into "productivity blog" fast. Keep it dark, keep it sparse, and keep the count under six objects.

### Direction C · The archive, drawn

**Subject.** No photography at all. A large, fine, single-weight isometric line drawing occupying the panel: a run of archive shelving in perspective, boxes on the shelves, one drawer or box pulled out and open, its contents implied by a few lines. Drawn in hairline `#8C887F` on a warm paper ground, on an almost invisible grid texture.

This is the device from Axiom's section two, promoted to hero scale.

**Strength:** it needs no photography, no image model, and no licensing. It is fully controllable, it can be produced as SVG so it stays crisp at any size and can be animated line by line, and line drawing is the most credible register there is for a technical buyer. It is also, of the three, the least likely to be mistaken for anyone else's page.
**Risk:** it is quieter. It will not stop a scroll the way Direction A will. Consider it the safe hedge, or the treatment for the second section rather than the hero.

## Part 4 · Production notes that will save a day of rework

**Do not ask an image model for the measurement overlay.** Image models produce approximately regular marks, which is worse than either regular or random. Generate the photograph clean, then build the overlay as **SVG or CSS on top in the page**, where it is crisp, controllable, themeable, and can be animated. Same for the hairline connectors in Direction B.

**Do not ask an image model for legible type.** It cannot do it. Either keep the type out of focus and illegible by design, as Direction A specifies, or composite a real rendered page in afterwards.

**Redaction bars go in last, as vectors.** Hard-edged pure black rectangles, drawn in post or in the page. A model will render them soft, or as grey, or with a bevel.

**Value-compress the subject before you judge the composite.** The Axiom lesson: pull the subject's contrast and saturation down toward the ground until it is almost too far, then bring it back one step. Most composites fail because the subject is too crisp, not because it is badly cut out.

**Reserve the type hole first.** Before generating, mark the region where the caption, card, or headline will sit and treat it as a constraint on the generation, not as something to fix afterwards.

**Grain last and globally.** Never per element.

**Generate wide, crop tight.** Ask for more frame than you need so the crop can be adjusted to the panel ratio without regenerating. Hero panels change width across breakpoints.

## Part 5 · Prompt scaffolds

These are starting points, not finished prompts. Expect eight to fifteen iterations. The paragraph in each that must not be softened is the one about light and value.

**Direction A, primary:**

> Editorial still life photograph. A small stack of printed A4 documents on a plain dark surface, shot from a low oblique angle roughly twenty degrees above the plane, top sheet slightly offset. Single hard directional light from the left at a shallow raking angle, revealing paper texture and a slight curl at the top sheet's edge. The right third of the frame falls into deep shadow, no fill light, no second source. The printed text is present but not legible, reading as fine grey texture at this distance. Warm off-white paper, never pure white. Near-black warm shadow. Entirely monochrome apart from one small warm ochre printed index tab on the edge of the stack. Fine monochrome film grain throughout, heavier in the shadows. Shot on a medium format camera with a 80mm lens at f/5.6. Muted, quiet, serious. Upper left third of the frame is empty shadow with no detail. No text overlays, no logos, no hands, no props, no glow, no lens flare, no colour grading.

**Direction A, alternate framing, closer:**

> Macro editorial photograph of the corner of a printed report, raking light from the left, extreme shallow depth of field so only a two centimetre band across the page is in focus. Paper fibre visible. The rest falls off into warm near-black. Monochrome except one thin ochre printed rule. Fine film grain. No legible text. Negative space in the upper left.

**Direction B:**

> Overhead flat lay photograph, camera directly above and parallel to the surface, no perspective distortion. Five printed documents arranged loosely on a dark warm surface, one turned ninety degrees to the others, one folded. Soft even overhead light, gentle shadow under the paper edges only. Desaturated to near monochrome, warm bias. One small ochre index tab. Fine film grain. Sparse composition with generous empty surface in the lower left. No hands, no people, no laptops, no coffee, no plants, no pens.

**Direction C** is drawn, not generated. Build it as SVG at a single stroke weight of 1px at display scale, isometric at thirty degrees, no fill, no shading, on a warm paper ground with a `repeating-linear-gradient` grid texture below five percent opacity.

## Part 6 · Acceptance checklist

Reject the image and regenerate if any of these fail.

- [ ] Exactly one hue family. No second chroma anywhere, including in the shadows.
- [ ] The reserved negative space is genuinely empty and low contrast. Type placed on it needs no scrim, no shadow, no box.
- [ ] Grain is present, monochrome, and consistent across every element.
- [ ] Nothing glows. No bloom, no flare, no rim light, no neon.
- [ ] No banned subject from Part 2 has crept in, including in the background.
- [ ] The subject is cropped by at least one frame edge, so it reads as larger than the frame.
- [ ] No legible fake text anywhere. Illegible by design, or composited real.
- [ ] Redaction bars, if present, are hard-edged and pure black.
- [ ] At 25 percent zoom the image still reads as one thing, not as elements on a background.
- [ ] Held next to the page's cream and navy, it belongs. Hold the actual page open next to it before approving.

## Part 7 · The one sentence to keep

Axiom's cloud works because it is **a thing being measured**, not a thing being described. Our equivalent is a document you cannot quite read, with the removed parts rendered as the only certainty in the frame. Everything in this brief exists to protect that idea from being decorated.
